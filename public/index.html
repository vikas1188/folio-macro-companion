How this estimate works +

CPI and unemployment have statistical baselines from FRED history. Jev separately assesses source evidence and may supply an experimental distribution when it finds enough evidence. Baseline estimates remain labeled when Jev abstains. Inflation and unemployment compare with the latest observation, not analyst consensus. Inventory outcomes use the weekly change reported in the next EIA release. These are experimental subjective estimates, not validated probabilities. The source selection is incomplete and no futures-market baseline is included.

Dollar impacts use fixed, illustrative one-week ETF returns for each outcome. We weight those scenario calculations by the selected estimate’s probabilities. This is scenario arithmetic, not an identified causal intervention. This is not a statistically estimated return distribution. The same release can produce a different reaction depending on expectations, revisions, and accompanying information. These independent scenario illustrations must not be summed across events.

01 / BAYESIAN PROBABILITY

A belief can change.
The rule stays explicit.

Start with a prior. Compare how likely the evidence is under an event and its alternative. Bayes’ rule gives the updated probability.

Posterior odds = prior odds × likelihood ratio

In Folio today: monthly forecasts use smoothed historical transition frequencies. Their shrinkage resembles an empirical Bayesian prior, but Reddit posts are not converted into measured likelihood ratios. Jev’s separate estimates are not a formal Bayesian news update.

02 / JUDEA PEARL’S DO OPERATOR

“We saw a rate cut.”
“What if we set one?”

Observing a decision and intervening on it are different questions. Pearl’s do() notation represents replacing a variable’s usual mechanism with an imposed value.

In Folio today: the map shows candidate connections and the calculator applies assumed returns. We do not yet estimate identified do() effects. That requires a defensible causal model, confounding assumptions, and validation; the notation alone cannot establish causation.

Illustrative causal graph · not a fitted model

Observe: rate decisions and asset returns can share causes, such as inflation. Watching rate cuts does not isolate what caused the return.

03 / YOUR PORTFOLIO, STEP BY STEP

Every dollar has a calculation.

For a chosen scenario, multiply each position by its assumed return and add the results. Probability weighting averages these scenario results; it does not turn assumed returns into causal effects.

Scenario P&L = Σ(position value × assumed return)

Rate-cut example: SPY +2%, TLT +3%, GLD +2%, SLV +2%, USO +1%, cash 0%. These one-week returns are assumptions, not measured intervention effects.

See assumptions & limitations +

These are hand-authored stress scenarios, not forecasts or live market data. No probability is assigned. Returns are fixed assumptions, not statistical confidence intervals. The calculation is the sum of each holding's weight multiplied by its assumed return.

The comparison transfers allocation from SPY to cash. Cash assumes zero return over the week. Taxes, fees, slippage, dividends, and intraperiod price movements are excluded. Lower losses in one scenario can mean lower gains in another.
